The Green Bay Packers entered Week 7 against the Detroit Lions with a puzzling decision: veteran tackle Kevin Zeitler was listed as a healthy scratch despite appearing fit. The offensive line had already shown vulnerability, surrendering 45.5% pressure on Jordan Love in the previous week’s loss to the Cowboys. Against Detroit, the line allowed 19 pressures, eight of which were charged to right guard Travis Glover. This pattern of underperformance raises concerns about the coaching staff’s approach to veteran talent and line versatility. How the Packers address these issues could shape their playoff chances.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Packers’ reluctance to play a healthy veteran on a struggling line is jeopardizing their offensive stability.</Callout>

## What happened
The team’s decision to keep Zeitler inactive came after a month without a single snap, even though his quadriceps hyperextension surgery recovery was reportedly complete. Earlier in the season, he signed to bolster the line but missed practice throughout the offseason, limiting his readiness.

In Week 6, the line allowed the Cowboys to pressure Love on 45.5% of his dropbacks. The following week against the Lions, the line surrendered 19 pressures, with right guard Travis Glover responsible for eight of them. The high pressure rate contributed to stalled drives and a loss that highlighted the line’s inconsistency.

## Why it matters
A porous offensive line directly affects quarterback protection and the running game, both critical for a team aiming for postseason success. Continuing to bench a healthy veteran like Zeitler may erode confidence among younger players and limit in‑game adjustments. Moreover, the pattern of over‑relying on positional versatility without proven performance could hinder the Packers’ ability to compete against elite defenses.

## How to think about it
Coaches should evaluate each lineman’s readiness on a per‑play basis rather than defaulting to a blanket health designation. Integrating Zeitler in limited snaps could provide immediate relief while allowing younger players to develop. Simultaneously, the staff must tighten gap discipline for guards like Glover, using film study and targeted drills to reduce pressure rates.

<Callout type="warning">Avoid making roster changes based solely on short‑term pressure statistics without considering overall health and development trajectories.</Callout>
