Luke Mittelstadt’s breakout month has put the Laval Rocket’s defense line in the spotlight. The 23‑year‑old was named the Upper Deck AHL Rookie of the Month for October after tallying five goals and 11 assists in just 11 games. His 16 points not only led all AHL rookies but also matched a record set by Derick Brassard in 2007. The award highlights a rapid transition from college hockey to professional impact. Fans and scouts alike are taking note of his two‑way play.

<Callout type="takeaway">Mittelstadt’s 16‑point October makes him the league’s top rookie scorer.</Callout>

## What happened
The AHL announced on November 6 that Rocket defenseman Luke Mittelstadt earned the Upper Deck Rookie of the Month honor for October. In 11 games he recorded five goals and 11 assists for a total of 16 points, the most by any rookie in the opening month since Derick Brassard posted 16 points in 12 games in October 2007.

Mittelstadt logged four separate three‑point games, beginning with three assists in a 6‑1 win over Syracuse on Oct. 3 and followed by a streak of goals in four consecutive contests from Oct. 10‑17, including an overtime winner at Belleville on Oct. 16. He extended his scoring streak to seven games with an assist on the game‑winning goal against Hamilton on Oct. 23 and added his fourth power‑play goal on Oct. 29 in Calgary.

Beyond raw numbers, he tied for second in the league with 11 assists, ranked fifth among rookies in plus/minus (+5), and contributed to Laval’s league‑leading power‑play efficiency (29.2%) and a strong penalty‑kill (88.2%).

## Why it matters
Mittelstadt’s production lifts the Rocket’s offensive ceiling while reinforcing their defensive reliability, a rare combination for a rookie defenseman. The performance not only improves Laval’s chances in the tight Eastern Conference race but also puts him on the radar of NHL scouts, potentially accelerating his path to a full‑time Montreal Canadiens role. For the AHL, his success underscores the league’s role as a proving ground for two‑way players who can impact both ends of the ice.
