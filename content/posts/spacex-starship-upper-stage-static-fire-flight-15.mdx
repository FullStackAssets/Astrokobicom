SpaceX has completed static‑fire tests of Starship’s upper stage, known as Ship 42, on Oct 26 and Oct 27. The six‑engine full‑duration burn and a single‑engine deorbit simulation demonstrate the vehicle’s readiness for Flight 15. This test follows a 33‑engine static fire of the Super Heavy booster, Booster 22, keeping the schedule on track for a November launch. Flight 15 aims to capture both stages with the launch‑tower “chopstick” arms, a first for the upper stage. Successful captures would validate SpaceX’s rapid‑reuse architecture.

<Callout type="takeaway">Flight 15’s dual‑stage capture hinges on these static‑fire milestones.</Callout>

## What happened
SpaceX ignited all six Raptor engines on Ship 42 for a full‑duration static fire on Oct 26, then performed a single‑engine burn on Oct 27 to simulate a deorbit maneuver. The company shared video of the tests on X, confirming that the upper stage performed as expected while anchored to the ground.

The upper‑stage tests came immediately after Booster 22 completed a 33‑engine static fire, confirming the Super Heavy first stage’s performance. With both stages validated, SpaceX remains on schedule for the November Flight 15 launch, which will attempt the first capture of the Ship stage using the tower’s chopstick arms.

## Why it matters
Capturing both stages would demonstrate a fully reusable launch system, dramatically reducing turnaround time and launch costs. Faster re‑flight cycles could enable more frequent Starlink V3 deployments, accelerating the rollout of a broadband constellation projected to reach 100,000 satellites.

Beyond commercial broadband, a reusable Starship architecture opens the door to more ambitious missions, from lunar landings to Mars cargo flights, by lowering the price per kilogram to orbit.

## How to think about it
Treat Flight 15 as a systems‑integration checkpoint: the static fires verify engine performance, the upcoming capture tests validate recovery hardware, and the launch timeline reflects regulatory and safety constraints. For enthusiasts, watching the live stream and monitoring X updates will provide real‑time insight into how each subsystem performs under flight conditions.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the capture will succeed; regulatory clearance and hardware reliability remain uncertain until the launch.</Callout>
