The Armstrong Flight Research Center, NASA’s hub for experimental aircraft, just received a new leader. On Oct. 29, 2026, NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman announced that veteran test pilot and aerospace engineer Troy Asher will serve as the center’s director. Asher, who has acted as director since March, brings decades of flight‑test experience to a program eager to rebuild its X‑plane fleet. His appointment signals a renewed push to expand high‑speed flight research and develop next‑generation aviation technologies.

<Callout type="takeaway">Troy Asher’s appointment positions NASA to accelerate its X‑plane program and modernize flight research.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman named Troy Asher director of the Armstrong Flight Research Center in Edwards, California. Asher, a former Air Force test pilot, has overseen flight operations at Armstrong and served as acting director since March.

In a statement, Isaacman praised Asher’s career of pushing the boundaries of flight and his ability to turn ambitious ideas into successful tests. Associate Administrator and Chief Engineer Amit Kshatriya highlighted Asher’s blend of technical excellence, operational judgment, and team trust.

As director, Asher will oversee the center’s workforce, aircraft fleet, and NASA’s effort to rebuild and expand the X‑plane portfolio, supporting both high‑speed flight and airborne science missions.

## Why it matters
The Armstrong Center is the agency’s Flight Test and Aircraft Operations Center of Excellence; its leadership directly influences how quickly new aerospace technologies move from concept to flight. With Asher’s deep operational background, NASA aims to restore a robust X‑plane lineup that has lagged for years, a step that could accelerate research on hypersonic vehicles, reusable launch systems, and advanced air‑breathing engines. Successful revitalization will keep the United States at the forefront of aeronautics and provide critical data for future lunar and Martian missions.
