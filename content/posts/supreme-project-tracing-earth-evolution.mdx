<Callout type="takeaway">Nearby supernovae have seeded Earth with heavy elements and shaped biological evolution over the past 500 million years, a story the SUPREME project is now unraveling.</Callout>

## What happened
The SUPREME project received a €3 million grant (≈$3.29 million) from the ERC, one of 319 groups selected for a total of €838 million (≈$918.86 million) to pursue breakthrough research.
The project focuses on two questions: first, how nearby supernovae have affected Earth and biological evolution since the Cambrian Period (≈538.8 to 485 million years ago); second, how the heaviest elements are created and distributed to Earth.
By measuring trace concentrations of heavy elements in sedimentary layers, the team will identify signatures of past supernova events and correlate them with geological and biological milestones.

## Why it matters
The timing of supernova enrichment could coincide with major evolutionary transitions, offering clues about the role of cosmic events in shaping life on Earth. Understanding nucleosynthesis pathways informs models of stellar evolution and the cosmic distribution of elements that form planets and life. The findings may also refine risk assessments for future nearby supernovae and their potential impact on Earth’s biosphere.

## How to think about it
Approach the SUPREME findings by considering the multi-disciplinary evidence: geochemical data, geological timelines, and biological records. Compare the timing of element spikes with known mass-extinction or diversification events. Use the data as a case study for how astrophysical processes can leave detectable fingerprints on planetary surfaces.

<Callout type="warning">The interpretation of heavy-element signatures is still evolving; avoid drawing definitive conclusions before peer review.</Callout>
