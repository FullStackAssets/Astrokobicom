On October 18, 1963, France launched its first and only feline astronaut, a stray tuxedo cat named Félicette, aboard a Véronique AG1 sub‑orbital rocket. The mission lifted the cat to roughly 130 miles above Earth before a parachute returned her safely to the ground. While the United States and Soviet Union focused on primates and humans, France’s experiment added a new species to the early space‑flight biological record. Félicette’s brief flight sparked public fascination and later inspired commemorations that keep the story alive today. The episode also raises enduring questions about the ethics of animal testing in space.

<Callout type="takeaway">Félicette’s flight proved that even small mammals could survive brief suborbital journeys, expanding the biological data pool of early spaceflight.</Callout>

## What happened
On 18 October 1963, the French Centre d'Enseignement et de Recherches de Médecine Aéronautique (CERMA) prepared a stray tuxedo cat named Félicette for a sub‑orbital launch. The cat replaced a fellow named Felix, who escaped on launch day, and was strapped into a capsule equipped with electrodes to record brain activity.

The Véronique AG1 rocket carried her to an altitude of about 130 miles before the capsule’s parachute deployed, allowing Félicette to descend and be recovered alive. She survived the flight, but two months later she was euthanized so scientists could examine the physiological data collected.

A second cat was launched on 24 October, but the flight veered off course and the animal died. The tragedy led CERMA to shift its focus to primates and later to other research. Decades later, a crowdfunded statue of Félicette was unveiled in 2019 at the International Space University in Strasbourg, and she appears on postage stamps from Chad and Comoros.

## Why it matters
Félicette’s flight broadened the biological dataset of early space missions by showing that small mammals could endure the stresses of launch, micro‑gravity, and re‑entry, informing later human and animal research. The mission also gave France a symbolic foothold in the Cold War space race, demonstrating technical capability and generating public enthusiasm. At the same time, the ethical implications of using animals for high‑risk experiments continue to influence contemporary standards for animal research in aerospace.

## How to think about it
When evaluating historic animal flights, weigh the scientific insights gained against the moral cost, and consider how modern ethical frameworks would reshape such experiments. Use Félicette’s story as a case study for how public interest, national ambition, and scientific curiosity intersect, and apply that lens to current debates about animal use in emerging technologies.
