NASA has opened a public naming opportunity for its Dragonfly rotorcraft that will explore Saturn’s moon Titan. Anyone can submit their name to be etched onto a chip that will travel with the probe, with submissions due Jan 15 2027. Dragonfly is slated to launch in 2028 and spend about 3.3 Earth years hopping across Titan’s surface after its 2034 arrival. The chance to be part of a mission that will search for pre‑biotic chemistry on a world with lakes and dunes makes the campaign a rare way for the public to connect with deep‑space exploration.

<Callout type="takeaway">Your name can ride a nuclear‑powered rotorcraft to Titan, but you must submit by Jan 15 2027.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced the “Name on Dragonfly” campaign on its events page in late October, inviting anyone to enter a name that will be stored on a microchip mounted on the spacecraft during assembly. The Dragonfly mission, a 2‑ton, nuclear‑powered rotorcraft, is targeted for launch in 2028, arrival at Titan in 2034, where it will conduct up to 20 flights across a 3‑year science campaign, sampling organic‑rich dunes, lakes, and cryovolcanic sites. The naming chip will be integrated during final integration, and NASA expects to receive thousands of entries before the Jan 15 2027 deadline, after which no further names will be added.

## Why it matters
Embedding public names on an interplanetary probe turns a distant scientific endeavor into a shared human experience, fostering broader interest in planetary science and encouraging the next generation of engineers. The campaign also highlights NASA’s commitment to outreach by linking everyday people to a mission that could answer fundamental questions about the chemistry of life beyond Earth. While the scientific payload remains unchanged, the personal connection can translate into increased support for future deep‑space exploration budgets.

## How to think about it
Treat the naming opportunity like a short‑term commitment: verify the deadline, keep your entry under 30 characters, and avoid special symbols that the system rejects. Record the confirmation email as proof of submission, and consider sharing the news with local schools or clubs to amplify the outreach impact. If you’re interested in deeper involvement, use the experience as a springboard to follow Dragonfly’s science updates and related citizen‑science projects.

<Callout type="warning">Do not wait until the last day; submissions after Jan 15 2027 will be rejected, and the mission timeline could shift if launch delays occur.</Callout>
