The holiday season can feel like a double‑edged sword for NASA employees, offering a chance to recharge but also bringing a surge of hidden stress. A recent NASA internal memo titled “MAF EAP – Holidays – How to manage the BAH HUMBUG that tries to sneak in” lays out a step‑by‑step approach to keep that stress from hijacking celebrations. The guidance blends simple self‑awareness with concrete tools such as calendar tracking and budget worksheets. Because burnout can ripple into mission performance, learning to tame the “BAH Humbug” matters for both personal well‑being and operational safety.

<Callout type="takeaway">The key to a calm holiday is to map your commitments, budget your time and money, and give yourself permission to say no.</Callout>

## What happened
The memo first asks readers to acknowledge their own feelings about the holidays, emphasizing that enjoying lights, shopping, or opting out are all valid choices. It encourages people to give themselves explicit permission to be authentic, which sets the tone for healthier decision‑making.

Next, it recommends merging personal and work calendars, listing every expected obligation, and tagging each item with a simple yes/no indicator. A parallel budget sheet tracks gifts, extra groceries, travel, utilities, and even higher electricity costs, with a reminder to keep receipts and involve the household in the numbers.

Finally, the guidance stresses realistic expectations: traditions may be non‑negotiable, but perfection is not required. When declining an invitation, it suggests using “I” statements focused on a single issue and always following up with a clear timeline for a response.

## Why it matters
Unchecked holiday stress can erode sleep, nutrition, and mental focus—critical factors for anyone working on high‑stakes aerospace projects. By externalizing commitments and costs, employees reduce the cognitive load that often leads to last‑minute scrambling or hidden resentment. The ripple effect reaches teams: a well‑rested, financially secure individual contributes more reliably to mission timelines and safety protocols.

## How to think about it
1. **Self‑check** – Before the season starts, write a brief note on what you truly enjoy and what drains you. 2. **Calendar sync** – Import work meetings, family events, and personal downtime into one view; label each as mandatory or optional. 3. **Budget sheet** – Create categories (gifts, food, travel, utilities) and assign a realistic ceiling; update with receipts weekly. 4. **Communication script** – Keep a short phrase ready, e.g., “I’d love to join, but I need to check my calendar and will get back by Thursday.” Use it to buy time and set boundaries.

<Callout type="warning">Do not let the checklist replace genuine connection; if you cancel every event, you may isolate yourself.</Callout>
