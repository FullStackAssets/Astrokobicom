On November 13, 2026, a moisture‑laden Pacific storm slammed the Sierra Nevada, delivering the first major snowfall of the water year. Snow accumulations reached roughly a foot at Donner Pass and up to 13 inches at Mammoth Mountain, while lower elevations saw heavy rain. The sudden whiteout forced the closure of Interstate 80 and prompted road shutdowns in Yosemite National Park. NASA’s Terra satellite captured the rapid transformation, showing bare peaks one day and fresh snow the next. Understanding this early‑season event matters for water resource planning and winter‑storm preparedness across California.

<Callout type="takeaway">The early November storm delivered a sudden, heavy snowpack that disrupted travel and signals an active winter ahead.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA’s MODIS instrument on the Terra satellite recorded the Sierra Nevada on the morning of November 14, revealing fresh snow that was absent just two days earlier. The storm deposited about 12 inches (30 cm) of snow at Donner Pass and 9–13 inches (23–33 cm) at Mammoth Mountain.

The heavy snowfall snarled traffic on Interstate 80, leading officials to close the highway in both directions that evening. Yosemite National Park also shut several roads, and the National Weather Service issued a winter storm warning for elevations above 5,000 feet, forecasting up to 4 feet (120 cm) of snow above 8,000 feet.

## Why it matters
An early, substantial snowpack adds to California’s seasonal water reservoir, offering a head start on water storage for agriculture and urban use. At the same time, sudden snow and rain increase the risk of travel disruptions, avalanches, and flooding, demanding rapid response from emergency managers. The event also provides a clear case study for how satellite imagery can track fast‑changing weather impacts in mountainous regions.

## How to think about it
Monitor updates from the National Weather Service and local transportation agencies before heading into the Sierras. If you must travel, allow extra time, carry chains, and stay informed about road status. Water managers should incorporate early snow measurements into reservoir planning to adjust allocations for the coming months.

<Callout type="warning">Do not attempt travel on closed highways or ignore winter storm warnings.</Callout>
