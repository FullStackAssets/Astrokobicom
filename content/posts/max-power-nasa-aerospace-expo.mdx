In early November, NASA opened its doors at the Kennedy Space Center for MAX POWER, the agency’s inaugural aerospace exhibition. Thousands of visitors streamed in over four days of air shows, aircraft displays, and lunar‑exploration briefings. The event celebrated America’s 250th anniversary and the upcoming Artemis II lunar flyby, while unveiling new recruitment drives and a student Moon Base competition. By gathering industry leaders, policymakers, and the public, MAX POWER signaled a renewed push toward a sustained lunar presence.

<Callout type="takeaway">MAX POWER marks NASA’s first large‑scale public showcase of its lunar‑focused roadmap and talent pipeline.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA’s MAX POWER ran from Nov. 4‑8 at Kennedy Space Center, featuring flyovers, a skydive by Administrator Jared Isaacman, and a visit from President Donald J. Trump. The multi‑day, family‑friendly expo highlighted rockets, landers, rovers, and research aircraft, with the U.S. Air Force Thunderbirds headlining the airshow. Attendees could explore exhibits from partners such as UP.Summit, Air Dot Show, and Purpose Entertainment, and hear moderated discussions from astronauts, engineers, and scientists.

Key announcements included opening astronaut applications through Dec. 3 for Artemis and low‑Earth‑orbit missions, a $1 million student competition to design innovative Moon Base solutions, and the selection of 12 companies for task orders advancing the first lunar outpost. The event also honored the nation’s 250th birthday and underscored NASA’s commitment to returning humans to the Moon and building a sustainable presence.

## Why it matters
MAX POWER serves as a public rallying point for NASA’s Artemis era, turning abstract mission goals into tangible experiences for citizens and potential workforce talent. The astronaut recruitment drive expands the pool of explorers needed for upcoming lunar and deep‑space flights, while the Moon Base challenge cultivates the next generation of engineers and designers. By showcasing new aircraft and autonomous technologies, the expo also signals commercial and defense partners that NASA’s vision is moving toward near‑term implementation, influencing funding priorities and international collaboration.

## How to think about it
If you’re a student, consider forming a team for the Moon Base challenge early to meet the design milestones before the competition deadline. Professionals interested in astronaut roles should review the eligibility criteria on NASA’s website and submit applications before Dec. 3, noting that the selection process is highly competitive. For anyone wanting to stay engaged, follow NASA’s Artemis updates, attend future public events, and explore volunteer or internship opportunities that align with the technologies highlighted at MAX POWER.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that all NASA job or competition deadlines are open indefinitely; missing the Dec. 3 astronaut application deadline or the Moon Base challenge submission window can forfeit eligibility.</Callout>
