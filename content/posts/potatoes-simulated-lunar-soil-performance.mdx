Imagine harvesting a fresh potato on the Moon. Researchers at Oregon State University have demonstrated that potatoes can complete their life cycle in simulated lunar regolith when the soil is amended with organic matter. By adding 30% vermicompost, the plants grew as robustly as those in Earth soil. This breakthrough tackles a key hurdle for long‑duration lunar habitats, where resupply from Earth is costly. The findings bring NASA’s Artemis food‑production goals a step closer to reality.
<Callout type="takeaway">Potatoes grown in lunar regolith simulants with 30% vermicompost match Earth‑soil yields.</Callout>
## What happened
The team grew potato plants in five different lunar regolith simulants representing mare and highland compositions. Each simulant was mixed with varying percentages of vermicompost—0%, 5%, 10%, 15% and 30%—and compared to a control Earth soil.
Plants grown in the 30% vermicompost mixes displayed growth rates, tuber size, and overall biomass indistinguishable from the Earth‑soil control. By contrast, the 0% mixes produced severely stunted plants with underdeveloped root systems, while the 5%‑15% mixes showed only partial recovery of growth metrics.
These results were published in npj Microgravity and underscore the importance of organic amendment for lunar agriculture.
## Why it matters
Achieving comparable yields means future lunar bases could rely on locally sourced regolith combined with a modest amount of organic material, dramatically cutting launch mass and cost. Recycling crew waste into vermicompost would create a self‑sustaining loop, supporting long‑term habitation and reducing dependence on Earth supply chains. The experiment also provides a scalable framework for testing other staple crops under lunar conditions.

## How to think about it
Start any lunar greenhouse with a baseline of about 30% vermicompost mixed into the regolith to ensure robust plant health. Implement waste‑to‑compost systems early to replenish the organic pool, and continuously monitor soil chemistry as the regolith composition may vary across sites. Integrate crop growth with life‑support systems to recycle water and nutrients, turning the greenhouse into a multifunctional habitat component.
<Callout type="warning">Do not attempt to grow crops in raw lunar regolith without adding organic matter, as plants will suffer severe stunting.</Callout>
