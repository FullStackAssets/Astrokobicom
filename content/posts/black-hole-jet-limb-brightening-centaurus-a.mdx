Opening paragraph\n\n<Callout type='takeaway'>Jets from supermassive black holes can shine brighter along their edges, a pattern first clearly seen in Centaurus A, revealing new physics of particle acceleration.</Callout>\n\n## What happened\n\nAstrophysicists have mapped the jet of the nearest radio galaxy, Centaurus A, with the Event Horizon Telescope and other VLBI arrays for over a decade. The maps show a consistent limb‑brightened structure, where the outer edges of the jet outshine its center. Recent modeling suggests that anisotropic particle acceleration in a magnetically dominated sheath can explain the observed brightness profile.\n\n## Why it matters\n\nUnderstanding why jets light up at their edges helps constrain the magnetic field geometry and particle acceleration mechanisms that power active galactic nuclei. These jets inject enormous energy into the intergalactic medium, regulating gas cooling and star formation on galaxy scales. A clearer picture of jet structure therefore informs models of galaxy evolution and feedback.\n\n<ProsCons>\n<Pros>\n<li>Provides a concrete mechanism for edge‑brightening.</li>\n<li>Matches high‑resolution VLBI data from Centaurus A.</li>\n<li>Links jet physics to galaxy‑scale feedback.</li>\n</Pros>\n<Cons>\n<li>Based on a single nearby radio galaxy.</li>\n<li>Requires complex magnetic‑field geometry that is hard to test.</li>\n<li>Uncertain how universal the mechanism is across AGN.</li>\n</Cons>\n</ProsCons>\n\n## How to think about it\n\nWhen evaluating jet observations, consider the role of magnetic field orientation, the relative speeds of the spine and sheath, and the energy distribution of accelerated particles. Comparing multi‑frequency VLBI data can reveal whether limb brightening is a common feature or a peculiarity of Centaurus A.\n\n## FAQ\n<FAQ>\n<Question q='What is limb brightening?'>Limb brightening is when the edges of a jet appear brighter than its center, suggesting a sheath of accelerated particles around a faster spine.</Question>\n<Question q='Why does the edge of the jet appear brighter?'>The edge contains a layer where magnetic fields guide particles to higher energies, producing more synchrotron emission than the inner spine.</Question>\n<Question q='How does this affect our understanding of galaxy evolution?'>If jets deposit energy preferentially along their edges, the way they heat and stir the surrounding gas changes, influencing star‑formation suppression in host galaxies.</Question>\n</FAQ>
