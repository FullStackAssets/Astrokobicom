Imagine a data center floating in orbit, crunching AI workloads while being powered entirely by sunlight. On 18 November 2026, Flexell Space of South Korea and France’s ALATYR announced a memorandum of understanding to explore the use of next‑generation ultralight solar cells for orbital data centers. The collaboration brings together Flexell’s CIGS and perovskite solar technology with ALATYR’s modular CITADEL architecture and BYLD robotic system. If successful, the partnership could reduce launch mass and enable continuous, high‑performance computing far above Earth’s atmosphere. This development signals a shift toward truly space‑based digital infrastructure.

<Callout type="takeaway">The partnership aims to make space‑based computing viable by marrying ultralight solar cells with modular orbital data centers.</Callout>

## What happened
Flexell Space and ALATYR signed an MoU to jointly assess how Flexell’s next‑generation solar cells could meet the power demands of orbital data centers. The agreement outlines an exchange of technical requirements, including power density, thermal conditions, and deployment constraints, followed by joint development and validation studies.

ALATYR’s CITADEL modular infrastructure separates the service life of the supporting structure from the compute hardware, allowing computing modules to be refreshed in orbit while the power, cooling, and support systems remain in place. The BYLD robotic system is designed for on‑orbit assembly, maintenance, and upgrades, making the architecture adaptable to evolving workloads.

Flexell is advancing solar cells based on copper indium gallium selenide (CIGS) and perovskite materials, focusing on lightweight, flexible panels that can be manufactured in large areas. These designs promise higher specific power and lower mass compared with traditional rigid panels, potentially fitting the tight volume and weight budgets of large orbital platforms.

## Why it matters
Reliable, efficient power generation is the linchpin for making orbital data centers viable. Ultralight solar cells could dramatically cut launch mass, lowering costs and enabling larger power budgets for AI‑intensive workloads that would otherwise be constrained by existing power solutions.

By decoupling infrastructure longevity from compute refresh cycles, ALATYR’s modular approach could keep space‑based hardware up‑to‑date without costly full‑system replacements. Combined with abundant solar energy, this creates a pathway for persistent, low‑latency services that sit closer to end users than terrestrial data centers.

The partnership also showcases a growing international collaboration in space energy technology, linking Korean material science expertise with French orbital infrastructure know‑how, which may accelerate standards and supply chains for future space‑based infrastructure.

## How to think about it
For industry stakeholders, treat this partnership as a proof‑of‑concept milestone rather than a commercial product launch. Track the technology readiness of Flexell’s CIGS and perovskite cells, and monitor ALATYR’s progress on the CITADEL platform to gauge when integration becomes feasible. Investors should consider the long development timelines typical of space hardware and look for incremental milestones, such as sub‑scale demonstrators in low Earth orbit. Policymakers can use this case to shape standards for power generation and debris mitigation for future orbital infrastructure.

<Callout type="warning">Both the solar cell technologies and the modular data‑center architecture are still in early development; premature deployment could face reliability and safety challenges.</Callout>
