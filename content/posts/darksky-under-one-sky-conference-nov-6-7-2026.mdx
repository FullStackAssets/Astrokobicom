Nighttime darkness is vanishing fast, with 99% of people in the U.S. and Europe now living under light‑polluted skies. To confront this, DarkSky International is hosting a virtual conference titled "Under One Sky" on November 6‑7, 2026. The 24‑hour event will bring together astronomers, entomologists, policymakers, and community advocates to share tools for preserving the night. By gathering a global audience online, the conference aims to turn concern into concrete action for dark‑sky protection.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Under One Sky conference offers a worldwide platform to coordinate practical solutions against light pollution.</Callout>

## What happened

DarkSky International, a worldwide group dedicated to combating light pollution, will hold its annual Under One Sky conference virtually on Nov 6‑7, 2026. The 24‑hour program features transformative talks, panels, and interactive workshops designed for anyone interested in protecting the night.

Sessions will span astronomy, ecology, urban planning, and astrotourism, with experts sharing the latest research, policy tools, and community‑level strategies. NASA’s recent map showing areas where night‑time lights are dimming will be highlighted as a hopeful sign of progress.

## Why it matters

Light pollution erodes cultural heritage, harms wildlife, and obscures the Milky Way for up to 80% of North Americans. By convening a global audience online, the conference lowers barriers to participation, enabling communities worldwide to adopt proven mitigation tactics. The collective knowledge exchange can accelerate policy adoption and inspire grassroots projects that restore dark skies for future generations.

## How to think about it

Start by checking your local night‑sky quality using the light‑pollution map linked by the conference. Identify one simple lighting change—such as installing shielded fixtures or reducing outdoor lighting hours—and implement it in your home or neighborhood. Then use the conference recordings and resources to expand your actions, sharing successes with local officials and fellow enthusiasts to build momentum.
