The Rockets offered him a training‑camp contract and assigned him the historic No. 35, a number that had seen only sporadic use in franchise history. Faried participated in preseason workouts but was waived before the regular season began, never appearing in an official game for the team. The brief episode sparked conversation among fans about the symbolism of jersey numbers and the Rockets’ roster strategy. ## What happened
In July 2019 the Rockets announced that former Denver forward Kenneth Faried had signed a non‑guaranteed contract to compete for a roster spot. The team assigned him jersey No. 35, a number previously worn only by a handful of role players in the franchise’s early years. Faried took part in the Rockets’ preseason schedule, showcasing his trademark hustle and mid‑range shooting.

Despite solid effort, the Rockets cut Faried in early October as they finalized a roster that emphasized three‑point shooting and veteran depth. Because he was released before the season opener, Faried never logged a regular‑season minute in a Rockets uniform, and the No. 35 jersey returned to the team’s inventory.

The episode received modest media coverage, largely focusing on the rarity of the number and the strategic shift the Rockets were pursuing under their front office.

## Why it matters
The decision to give Faried No. 35 was more than a simple roster move; it signaled the Rockets’ willingness to experiment with veteran depth while honoring a lesser‑used number. For fans, jersey numbers serve as identity markers, and reviving an obscure number can generate merchandise interest and nostalgic conversation. From a management perspective, the move illustrated how non‑guaranteed contracts allow teams to test fit without long‑term commitment, a practice increasingly common in the salary‑cap era.
