Astronomers using the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) have captured an unprecedented edge‑on view of the protoplanetary disk around the young star known as Gomez’s Hamburger (GoHam). The observations reveal distinct vertical layers of gas and dust, and hint at the earliest stages of giant planet formation far from the central star. Because the disk is tilted almost exactly edge‑on, scientists can directly measure how material is stacked and moving, a perspective that is rarely available.

<Callout type="takeaway">GoHam provides the clearest edge‑on laboratory for watching giant planets take shape.</Callout>

## What happened
ALMA mapped millimeter‑sized dust and several molecular lines, including 12CO, 13CO, CS, and SO. The data show the lightest gas (12CO) highest above the midplane, 13CO slightly lower, and CS closest to the midplane, confirming theoretical expectations of vertical stratification.

The disk spans an extreme radius and displays strong asymmetries and wind signatures that may be linked to nascent planets carving gaps or stirring the gas. The combination of size, structure, and chemistry makes GoHam a benchmark system for testing detailed planet‑formation models.

## Why it matters
Understanding how giant planets can form at large orbital distances challenges traditional core‑accretion models that rely on dense, inner‑disk material. The vertical view offered by GoHam lets researchers test how dust settles, how gas chemistry evolves, and how emerging planets reshape their surroundings. Insights from this single system can refine simulations that aim to reproduce the diversity of exoplanetary systems observed elsewhere.

## How to think about it
When evaluating planet‑formation theories, treat edge‑on disks like GoHam as “ground‑truth” laboratories: compare model predictions of vertical temperature, density, and chemical gradients against the observed molecular stratification. Complement this with observations of disks at other inclinations to build a three‑dimensional picture of how giant planets emerge across different environments.

<Callout type="warning">Do not extrapolate the specific vertical structure of GoHam to all disks; orientation and local conditions can produce unique features.</Callout>
