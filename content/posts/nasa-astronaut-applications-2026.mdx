NASA has opened its latest astronaut recruitment, inviting U.S. citizens to apply for a class that will train for lunar base operations and the agency’s first crewed missions to Mars. The application window closes at 11:59 p.m. EST on Dec 3, 2026, and the final selection is slated for late 2027. This round comes as NASA prepares to expand the Moon’s permanent presence and lay groundwork for interplanetary travel. Prospective explorers will join a legacy that began in the 1960s and now includes more than 370 trained astronauts. The opportunity promises both personal adventure and a role in shaping humanity’s future in space.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA is recruiting its next astronaut class to support lunar base operations and pave the way for crewed Mars missions.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced that applications are now being accepted for its next astronaut class, with a deadline of Dec 3, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EST. The program is open only to U.S. citizens who meet the agency’s medical, education, and experience criteria.

Selected candidates will undergo training focused on lunar surface activities, long‑duration habitation on the planned Moon Base, and the technical skills needed for future Mars expeditions. NASA expects to announce the final roster in late 2027, after which the new class will begin a multi‑year training pipeline.

The previous class, announced in 2025, was chosen from more than 8,000 applicants and currently includes ten astronauts who are completing two years of training. Since the 1960s, NASA has selected roughly 370 individuals to become astronaut candidates, reflecting the agency’s evolving mission profile.

## Why it matters
Expanding the astronaut corps is essential for sustaining a permanent lunar presence, which serves as a testbed for life‑support systems, in‑situ resource utilization, and crew health protocols needed for Mars. A larger, diverse pool of explorers accelerates technology development and maintains U.S. leadership in deep‑space exploration. Moreover, the visibility of new astronaut selections inspires students and engineers, feeding the talent pipeline that will power future missions.

## How to think about it
If you are considering applying, start by reviewing NASA’s official eligibility criteria and assembling required documentation well before the deadline. Focus on demonstrating relevant technical experience, leadership, and problem‑solving abilities, as these are core to the selection process. Use the time before Dec 3 to seek mentorship from current or former astronauts, engage in simulated mission exercises, and ensure your medical evaluations are current. Treat the application as a long‑term career investment rather than a quick entry point; the training pipeline will shape your future contributions to lunar and Martian exploration.

<Callout type="warning">Submitting an incomplete or late application will result in automatic disqualification.</Callout>
